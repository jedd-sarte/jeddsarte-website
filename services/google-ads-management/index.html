Google Ads Management

What I manage

The goal is not to maximize activity inside the account. The goal is to build a search program that attracts the right demand, measures meaningful actions, and gives the business enough signal to make better budget decisions.

01 • STRUCTURE

Campaign architecture

Search campaigns organized around service lines, intent, geography, budget control, and landing page relevance instead of one oversized catch-all campaign.

02 • INTENT

Keyword and query control

Keyword research, match-type decisions, search term mining, negatives, and message alignment designed to protect budget from low-intent traffic.

03 • MEASUREMENT

Conversion tracking

Primary conversion actions are defined around real business outcomes, with GA4, GTM, enhanced conversions, or offline signals used when appropriate.

04 • BIDDING

Bid strategy

Bidding is selected based on data quality and account maturity. Automation is useful when the conversion signal is trustworthy, not simply because Google recommends it.

05 • ADS

Ad copy and testing

Responsive search ads built around actual buyer intent, differentiators, objections, and clear next steps, with testing tied to conversion quality.

06 • OPTIMIZATION

Ongoing account improvement

Budget allocation, search terms, impression share, CPA, ROAS, lead quality, conversion rate, and landing page alignment are reviewed as one system.

Before we work together

What does a Google Ads specialist manage?

Account structure, keyword research, search terms, bidding, ad copy, conversion tracking, landing page alignment, testing, budget allocation, and ongoing optimization should all work together. I treat them as one acquisition system rather than isolated account tasks.

Do you work with existing Google Ads accounts?

Yes. I can begin with an audit and prioritized action plan, then restructure or optimize the existing account based on what the data supports.

Do you only work with one industry?

No. My experience includes legal, home services, e-commerce, real estate, B2B, hospitality, and professional services. The common thread is measurable acquisition, not one specific vertical.
