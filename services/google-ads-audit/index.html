Google Ads Audit • U.S. Accounts

Find what is costing the account before you spend more.

A Google Ads audit should do more than list settings. I review the account as an acquisition system, from search intent and campaign structure to conversion tracking, bidding, landing pages, and whether Google is learning from the right outcomes.

What the review looks for

  • Search-term leakage and weak intent control
  • Campaign and budget structure problems
  • Conversion actions that distort bidding
  • Tracking gaps or duplicate signals
  • Ads that do not match the query or offer
  • Landing-page friction and relevance gaps
  • Automation being used before the data is ready
Audit Coverage

A full-account review, not a vanity-metric scorecard

The purpose of the audit is to identify the highest-impact problems first. A technically imperfect account can still work. A technically clean account can still waste money if it attracts the wrong demand or optimizes toward low-quality conversions.

01 • STRUCTURE

Campaign architecture

Review how campaigns, ad groups, budgets, locations, and service lines are separated and whether the structure creates useful control.

02 • SEARCH TERMS

Keyword intent

Check keywords, match types, negatives, and actual search queries for spend that is drifting away from the business's highest-value demand.

03 • TRACKING

Conversion quality

Validate which actions are primary, whether they represent meaningful outcomes, and whether bidding is being trained on noisy or duplicated signals.

04 • BIDDING

Automation readiness

Review bid strategy against conversion volume, conversion quality, budget, and account maturity rather than treating automation as a default answer.

05 • ADS

Message alignment

Compare ads with the query, offer, differentiators, and landing page so the account is not paying for intent it fails to address after the click.

06 • ECONOMICS

Budget and outcomes

Look at CPA, ROAS, conversion rate, impression share, lead quality, and revenue signals where available to identify where the next dollar should go.

Audit Process

From account symptoms to prioritized actions

The output should make it clear what needs attention now, what can wait, and what should not be changed without better data.

  1. 1. Establish the business goal

    Define the conversion or downstream outcome the account should actually optimize toward.

  2. 2. Review the traffic path

    Follow search query → keyword → ad → landing page → conversion signal and look for breaks in relevance or measurement.

  3. 3. Separate critical issues from optional improvements

    Prioritize problems that affect wasted spend, conversion quality, or Google's ability to optimize.

  4. 4. Build the action order

    Turn the findings into a practical sequence instead of changing everything in the account at once.

FAQ

Before requesting an audit

What is included in a Google Ads audit?

I review campaign structure, keywords, search terms, negatives, bidding, ad messaging, budget allocation, conversion tracking, landing-page alignment, and whether the account is optimizing toward meaningful business outcomes.

When should a business get a Google Ads audit?

Common triggers are stalled performance, weak lead quality, rising spend without a clear reason, uncertain tracking, a planned budget increase, or the need for an independent review before changing strategy or providers.

Can the audit be separate from management?

Yes. It can be a standalone second opinion or the first step before restructuring and ongoing management.

Second Opinion

An audit can clarify that before you make a large structural change or increase budget.
