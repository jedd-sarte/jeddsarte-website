Common legal paid search problems

  • Expensive clicks with weak lead quality
  • Broad queries consuming limited budget
  • Calls or forms counted without quality feedback
  • Campaigns split in ways that weaken control
  • Landing pages that do not match search intent
  • Bidding optimized toward the wrong conversion action
Strategy

Law firm paid search needs tighter intent control

The account should make it easier to answer a basic question: which searches, ads, locations, and landing pages are producing the strongest opportunities for the firm? That requires more than a high CTR or a low CPC.

01 • SEARCH INTENT

Separate high-value legal demand

Campaign and ad group structure should reflect the services and case types the firm actually wants, with keyword and negative controls protecting budget from irrelevant searches.

02 • GEOGRAPHY

Control where spend goes

Location targeting, service areas, exclusions, and campaign budgets should reflect the markets the firm can serve and the economics of each geography.

03 • CONVERSION QUALITY

Measure more than form fills

A lead count is useful only when the conversion action is meaningful. Where possible, the account should learn from qualified leads, consultations, retained cases, or other downstream outcomes.

04 • LANDING PAGES

Match the query to the next step

Someone searching for a specific legal service should land on a page that addresses that problem directly, builds trust, and makes the next action clear.

05 • BIDDING

Use automation with guardrails

Smart bidding can work well when the conversion signal is strong. If the signal is noisy, automation can simply scale the wrong outcome faster.

06 • REPORTING

Judge the business result

CTR, CPC, impression share, and conversion rate matter, but they should support a bigger view of lead quality, acquisition cost, signed business, and revenue when that data is available.

FAQ

Google Ads for legal practices

What matters most in Google Ads for law firms?

Query intent, geographic control, conversion quality, landing page alignment, and reliable tracking. Legal clicks can be expensive, so the account needs to protect budget and learn from meaningful outcomes instead of chasing traffic for its own sake.

Can you audit an existing law firm account?

Yes. I can review structure, keywords, search terms, bidding, conversion tracking, budget allocation, ads, landing pages, and whether the account is optimizing toward the right action.

Do you focus only on personal injury?

No. Personal injury is one area where I have direct experience, but the same disciplined approach to intent, tracking, and lead quality can be adapted to other legal and professional service campaigns.
