E-commerce Acquisition

Build the account around products people actually buy

Strong e-commerce optimization depends on clean purchase data and enough structure to control where spend goes without fragmenting the account beyond what the data can support.

01 • PURCHASE DATA

Revenue tracking

Confirm purchases, transaction values, currencies, and deduplication so bidding and reporting are working from trustworthy order data.

02 • SEARCH

High-intent demand

Use search campaigns where product or category intent is strong and message-match can improve conversion efficiency.

03 • SHOPPING

Product feed alignment

Review product titles, categories, identifiers, availability, and campaign grouping so Shopping traffic reflects the store's real catalog priorities.

04 • ECONOMICS

ROAS with context

Evaluate revenue, CPA, ROAS, average order value, and product economics together instead of treating every sale as equally valuable.

05 • BUDGET

Product allocation

Move budget toward proven categories, products, and search themes while limiting spend where demand or economics do not support scale.

06 • LANDING

Store experience

Ad traffic should land on relevant product or collection pages with strong mobile usability, clear pricing, availability, and checkout paths.

FAQ

Google Ads for e-commerce

What matters most for e-commerce Google Ads?

Purchase tracking, product economics, feed quality, search intent, budget allocation, and conversion-focused landing pages. Revenue data needs to be reliable before automated bidding can be trusted.

Should every store use Shopping campaigns?

No single campaign type is mandatory. Shopping can be valuable when the catalog, product data, margins, and conversion signal support it. The best mix depends on how customers search and what products the store needs to grow.
